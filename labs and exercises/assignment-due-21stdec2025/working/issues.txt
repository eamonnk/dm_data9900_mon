Comparison with Other Research and Reflections

Several existing studies have applied sentiment analysis techniques to the McDonald’s reviews dataset, providing useful points of comparison for this work.

Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis

York Yong attempted to apply aspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA) using transformer-based language models, including LLaMA, to identify sentiment related to specific aspects such as service, food quality, and cleanliness. While this approach aims to provide more granular insight, the implementation was not fully successful, highlighting the complexity of reliably extracting aspect-level sentiment from short, informal review text.

In contrast, the present study focused on overall sentiment classification, rather than aspect-level sentiment. This decision was driven by the nature of the dataset, where explicit references to aspects are often inconsistent or implicit. The comparison highlights that linear models combined with TF–IDF features are effective for global sentiment classification, but may be insufficient for aspect-based tasks without additional domain-specific feature engineering or annotation.

This suggests that aspect-based sentiment analysis remains a promising avenue for future work, potentially through the identification of domain-specific keywords or the use of larger, labelled datasets. However, such approaches would require more complex modelling techniques and additional data preparation.

Store-Level Sentiment Aggregation

Another study calculated store-level sentiment scores by aggregating review sentiment per location and visualising results geographically using latitude and longitude data. While this provides useful spatial insight into customer experience, the present study did not explicitly compute a single sentiment score per store.

Instead, this work prioritised distributional sentiment analysis through Likert-scale visualisation, allowing comparison of sentiment profiles across stores without collapsing information into a single metric. Nonetheless, the availability of store_id, latitude, and longitude data indicates a clear opportunity to extend the analysis by incorporating store-level sentiment scoring and spatial visualisation in future iterations.

Use of Transformer-Based Language Models

Several researchers explored the use of transformer-based language models, such as LLaMA, either through fine-tuning or prompt-based inference. These models leverage attention mechanisms to evaluate relationships between all words in a sentence simultaneously and can capture complex contextual dependencies.

While such approaches are powerful, they introduce significant computational overhead and reduce interpretability. In contrast, the linear models used in this study achieved excellent classification performance (ROC AUC ≈ 0.96) with substantially lower complexity and greater transparency. Given the size and structure of the dataset, transformer-based models were considered unnecessary for the task of binary sentiment classification.

However, with access to larger datasets or labelled aspect-level annotations, transformer-based approaches could offer additional benefits and warrant further investigation.

Reflections and Lessons Learned

The comparison with existing research highlights several key lessons. First, simpler linear models can achieve strong performance when paired with appropriate text representations such as TF–IDF. Second, interpretability remains a critical consideration, particularly in applied sentiment analysis tasks where understanding linguistic drivers is valuable. Finally, more complex modelling approaches do not necessarily yield superior results unless the dataset and task justify their use.

Overall, this study demonstrates that careful feature engineering, interpretability-focused analysis, and robust evaluation can provide meaningful insights without resorting to unnecessary model complexity.

Text Preprocessing Pipeline

Prior to feature extraction and modelling, review text was preprocessed to standardise input, reduce noise, and improve model performance while maintaining interpretability.

All reviews were lowercased to avoid duplicate tokens caused by case differences. Punctuation and non-alphabetic characters were removed, as they do not typically convey sentiment information in this context. Text was then tokenised into individual word tokens suitable for vectorisation.

Stopwords (e.g. “the”, “and”, “is”) were removed to reduce dimensionality and focus the models on sentiment-bearing terms. Lemmatisation was applied instead of stemming to reduce words to their base form while preserving semantic meaning (e.g. “running” → “run”). This choice supports later interpretability, particularly for log-ratio analysis and explanation of model outputs.

Overall, this preprocessing pipeline balances noise reduction, computational efficiency, and interpretability, aligning with the CRISP-DM emphasis on well-justified data preparation.
